## The short version

**Verrath did not invent the geometric rune notation. He found it.**

The Zone 4 anchor chamber and its node are not part of the Weave Accumulation Lattice — they predate it. Verrath's Zone 3 construction is scaffolding built _around_ something that already existed, already worked, and was already old when he found it. He is not the architect of the whole system. He is the second builder — the first person in a very long time to actually finish the job the first builder started, or abandoned, or was stopped from completing.

Call the original notation and its network **the Old Weave**.

---

## What this buys the campaign

- **Kyron's "not from a book, from the inside" moment now has a real object to eventually resolve to** — whatever his backstory turns out to connect him to, it can plausibly be the Old Weave itself rather than Verrath's work. You don't have to decide what that connection is yet; you now just need it to be consistent with "predates Verrath, predates the Arcanum's suspended research, possibly predates the city."
- **The "suspended eleven years ago" Arcanum research (already established) becomes a rediscovery, not an invention.** Someone at the Arcanum got close to the Old Weave once before, eleven years ago, and it was buried rather than published. Worth deciding later whether Verrath was involved in that research himself, or found out about it after and went looking for what got suppressed.
- **Precedent for other anchor points.** If the Old Weave is a network, Caer Hollow's node doesn't have to be the only one. Free hooks for later — other cities, ruins, or (very usable post-Blackening) Shadowfell-side mirrors of the same notation, which would land hard if the party recognizes it there.
- **Reframes Verrath slightly, in a good way.** He's not "mad genius who invented ley-compression architecture from nothing." He's an ambitious, corrupted man who recognized what he'd found and was willing to finish it. Consistent with the existing "true believer, not thrall" characterization — he didn't need to be a genius inventor, just clever and desperate enough to use something that was always loaded.

## Open question, not yet decided

Who/what built the Old Weave. Leading (not locked) option: it's connected to whatever built or used the Book of Nagash network already planned for the Shadowfell arc — meaning the notation is, in some sense, an older incursion's leftover architecture, and Verrath found a scar rather than started fresh. If you go this way, the "suspended eleven years ago" research becomes the _second_ time in recorded history someone got close to this, not the first, which is a nice piece of dramatic irony to plant now and cash in later.

You don't need to resolve this before Session 18. Nothing about the session below depends on the answer — it only needs the _shape_ (old, predates Verrath, predates the sewers) to hold.

## The suspended research, eleven years ago — real answer

Your players are going to ask what the research actually was the moment Felwin mentions it exists. Here's the answer, DM-only.

**The Undertow Programme.** An Arcanum Deep Research Index project from eleven years ago, studying anomalous ley readings beneath Caer Hollow — the same kind of elevated, oddly-directed readings that show up again in your current session content. The programme's working theory was that something beneath the city was drawing and redirecting ambient ley energy on its own, and the goal was to understand — and eventually control — that flow. In other words: this was the first serious attempt to study the Old Weave, not a Verrath invention at all. The Arcanum found the node before Verrath did. They just didn't know what they'd found.

**Verrath was on the team.** Junior scholar, not yet High Arcanist. This is the important part — it means Verrath isn't discovering the anchor chamber fresh in the current campaign timeline. He's _returning_ to it. Whatever obsession eventually made him vulnerable to Nagash may well have started here, over a decade earlier, as unfinished work he was never allowed to go back to.

**What ended it:** a field test at the anchor site went wrong. A containment failure, a death (pick a name if useful — a mentor figure Verrath was close to is a strong option, and gives you a personal wound to reference later without having to invent much). Rather than let news of an Arcanum death and a genuinely dangerous piece of ley-architecture reach the public, the programme was suspended, its findings classified, and its remaining researchers quietly reassigned or sidelined. The official record calls it "excessive volatility, suspended pending further theoretical safeguards." That's true as far as it goes. It omits that someone died and that the Arcanum chose reputation management over honesty about it.

**Who signed off on burying it:** **Overseer Haeln** — already an established NPC (the procedural bureaucrat from Merios's custody hearing and Samson's scholarship review). He was administrative staff on the Undertow Programme's oversight committee eleven years ago, long before either of those later scenes. He's the one who filed the paperwork that made the death disappear into "volatility." He may not even remember Verrath specifically from back then — it was one project among many he's signed off on over a long career of making inconvenient things quiet. That's the point: to Haeln this was routine institutional housekeeping. To Verrath it was formative. Reusing Haeln here is optional but efficient — you already have a face and a voice for him, and it turns a background bureaucrat into something quietly unsettling in hindsight the next time players see him.

This does not need to come out in Session 18. Felwin's version below stops well short of it. Treat the Verrath-was-on-the-team detail and the Haeln connection as a later, heavier reveal — ideally one the party has to dig for rather than one Felwin simply hands them.

## What is and isn't sayable in-fiction right now

**Provable from the dungeon itself, no speculation required:**

- Zone 4's stonework and the node are older than Zone 3's construction — already established via Investigation DC 13/17 in the existing dungeon note.
- The notation style in Zone 3 (Verrath's Lattice) visually derives from something rougher and older, first glimpsed as "preliminary notation" scratches back in U1-B.

**Felwin's working theory (see Session 18 note) — flagged in her own dialogue as speculation, not fact:**

- The suspended Arcanum research was a rediscovery, not an original breakthrough.
- Whoever built the anchor wasn't building a weapon — it reads more like infrastructure. Verrath may be the first person in a long time willing to actually use it for what it was for.

**Not sayable by anyone yet, including Felwin:**

- Who built it, when, or its connection (if any) to Nagash. Felwin should not get close to this. If a player asks her directly, her honest answer is "I don't know" — and that should read as a real gap in her knowledge, not coyness.

## If you want to seed this further before Session 18 is over

Small, deniable touches that don't commit you to anything:

- A single rune sequence in Zone 4 that doesn't match anything else in the room stylistically — older-looking even than the rest of the anchor chamber. No mechanical effect. Just something for a sharp-eyed player to remember.
- Felwin's reveal scene (see Session 18 note) is a good place to plant the idea that this notation might exist elsewhere, without saying where — she'd frame it as "if this pattern is real infrastructure and not a one-off, there should be more of it somewhere," which is exactly the kind of thing a careful researcher would say without proof.
