## Closing Scene — Felwin's Home

_Replaces the "Exit + Felwin debrief (15 min)" line from Session 17's pacing table. Budget 20–30 minutes for this properly — it's the informational reward, not a wrap-up line._

### Setup

Felwin doesn't debrief them at the sewer exit. She asks them back to her home instead — a request, not a given; if the party doesn't push for more than the gold, she'll let the moment pass with only a brief thanks and payment, and this scene doesn't happen. **This is the "slightly push Felwin" gate you described** — make it a real choice, not a formality. A single line of "we'd like to hear what you've pieced together" or similar is enough to unlock it; she's not going to make them work hard for it once they're this far in, she's been waiting for someone to ask.

### The room

> _Her home is smaller than you'd expect for someone with her access — a narrow townhouse in the Scholar's Descent, unremarkable from the street. The room she leads you to is not. Every wall is covered: maps pinned edge to edge, string running between marked points, scraps of notation copied in her own hand, dates written in the margins of things that shouldn't have margins. A working desk sits in the middle, buried under more of the same. This is not a tidy conspiracy board — it's six weeks of someone doing real, careful, increasingly frightened work alone._

Let this room breathe. It's the visual reward as much as anything she says — the party should be able to look at it and roughly follow the shape of her investigation even before she opens her mouth: the sewer map from Session 15, string running to a marked point at Cradle Tower, the keystone rubbing from Zone 1→2, a copy of the construction ledger's institutional letterhead.

### What she shares

Present this as a conversation, not a monologue — let players ask, and let her answer some things readily and visibly hold back on others. She is generous here because they've earned it, not because she's stopped being careful.

**She'll confirm plainly, without hedging:**

- Verrath's name and division, now that she has physical evidence rather than just a suspicion she couldn't act on
- That the relay network converges on Cradle Tower
- That the underlying architecture matches research the Arcanum suspended eleven years ago — and that she believes that was a rediscovery, not an original finding, though she can't yet prove whose
- **If asked what the research actually was** (expect this question): _"It has a name, on paper — the Undertow Programme. Deep Research Index, same division Verrath answers to now. Officially it studied irregular ley readings under the city. Officially, it was suspended for 'excessive volatility, pending further theoretical safeguards.'"_ She can produce this from Arcanum public filings and cross-referenced library records — this is confirmed fact for her, not guesswork, and she'll state it as such.

**Her working theory, explicitly flagged as theory:**

- _"I don't think he built this. I think he found it, and finished it."_ — she'll say this if pressed on the age mismatch between Zone 3 and Zone 4, but frame it honestly as inference from the stonework, not certainty.
- _"If this is real infrastructure and not a single, isolated thing — there should be more of it somewhere else."_ She has no location for this. It's a hypothesis born from the way the network is built, not a lead.
- On the Undertow Programme: she suspects — but cannot prove — that "excessive volatility" is a polite word for something that went badly wrong, and that the suspension was about controlling what got out, not just caution. She has no name for who made that call, and doesn't claim to.

**What she genuinely doesn't know and will say so:**

- Who or what originally built the notation system, or when. This should read as a real gap in her knowledge, not reticence. Let her be visibly frustrated by this rather than mysterious about it.
- Whether anyone specific from the Undertow Programme still works within the Arcanum, let alone whether Verrath himself was ever connected to it. This hasn't occurred to her yet — don't let her get close to it. It's a later, heavier reveal, not something she can stumble into from library records.
- What Crownwatch's involvement means beyond "someone official already knows this place exists." She wants the Crownwatch sigil back if the party is willing to give it up — or at minimum wants to examine it here, now, before deciding what to do.

**What she still won't do:**

- Push her "buried, not abandoned" suspicion any further than stated above, even under pressure — because she genuinely doesn't have more. If a player asks her to name a suspect, the honest answer is that she doesn't have one yet, and she'll say so rather than perform reluctance she doesn't feel.

### Payment and parting

Gold as originally planned. In addition, she gives them something tangible from the wall — your call what fits the table, options in order of weight:

- A copy of her full sewer/relay map, string and all, redrawn for them to keep
- Standing invitation to use this room going forward — she'd rather have allies who bring her evidence than keep working alone
- If the party surrendered the cipher document from the Zone 4 lockbox to her last session, she can now report she's made partial headway on it — not solved, but no longer opaque. A good dangling thread for Session 19.

### Close

> _She doesn't walk you out so much as simply stop talking, the way someone does when they've said more than they meant to and needs a moment to sit with that. "Thank you," she says, finally. "Truly. I've been doing this alone for a long time." A pause. "Be careful who you tell."_
